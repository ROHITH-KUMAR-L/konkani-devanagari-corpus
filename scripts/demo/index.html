Project overview

A Konkani corpus, and NLP built on it

Konkani, the official language of Goa, has almost no usable digital text. We built a clean Konkani (Devanagari) corpus, measured it, and used it to train language tools.

Done

Is it Konkani?

Phase 1 · Cleaning, iteration 2 · validate_konkani.py

What it is
The filter that keeps Marathi and Hindi out of our Konkani corpus. All three languages use the same Devanagari script, so the computer cannot tell them apart by the letters alone.
How it works
Every paragraph is scanned for small, very common words that only one language uses: Konkani आसा, आनी, हांव; Marathi आहे, आणि, मी; Hindi है, और, में. If at least 2 Marathi/Hindi markers appear and they outnumber the Konkani ones, the paragraph is dropped. It also drops OCR garbage (words that start with a vowel sign, or more than 15% broken words) and number tables.
NLP concept
Language identification, rule-based with marker words. A random check of 200 final paragraphs found only 1% non-Konkani left.

Sentence splitter

Phase 1 · Sentence segmentation (brief step 8) · segment_sentences.py

What it is
Cuts running text into sentences, one per line, which every later model needs.
How it works
A full stop is not always the end of a sentence. The rules skip abbreviations (डॉ., श्री.), initials (डी., any one-syllable word), decimals (3.5) and list numbers, and start a new sentence at a newspaper dateline such as मडगांव:. Under each sentence, words are shown split into aksharas (written syllables), the unit we use for word length.
NLP concept
Sentence boundary detection, measured with precision, recall and F1 against a hand-checked test set: F1 0.990 on 154 paragraphs it never saw during development.

How models see words

Phase 2 · Tokenisation · SentencePiece models trained on our corpus

What it is
Shows how a sentence is broken into the units a model actually reads: whole words, or smaller learned pieces.
How it works
A whole-word model only knows words it saw in training; 4–5% of words in new text are unseen. Subword tokenisers (BPE and Unigram) learned frequent pieces from our corpus, so any word, even a new one, can be built from pieces such as घर + ांत. ▁ marks the start of a word. Pieces per word (fertility) shows the cost: smaller vocabularies cut words into more pieces.
NLP concept
Subword tokenisation (BPE, Unigram LM), vocabulary size, out-of-vocabulary words. Important for Konkani because grammar is glued onto words.

Next-word suggestions

Phase 2 · N-gram language model · counted on our 8.5M-word training split

What it is
A phone-keyboard style autocomplete for Konkani.
How it works
The model counted every sequence of 3 words in the training text. Given the last two words you typed, it suggests the words that most often came next (trigram). If that context is rare, it falls back to the last word only (bigram, weight 0.4), then to the most common words (weight 0.16); this is called stupid backoff. If you stop in the middle of a word, it completes that word instead.
NLP concept
Language modelling with n-grams: predicting the next word from the previous ones. The same idea, with proper smoothing (Kneser-Ney), gives our best perplexity of 484.
Try this
Type मुख्यमंत्री प्रमोद and a space (→ सावंत), or type कोंक. Click a suggestion to add it. The ⌨ keyboard at the bottom right uses the same model.

Word meaning from vectors

Phase 2 · Word embeddings · FastText trained on our corpus

What it is
Finds words with similar meaning, learned only from how words are used, with no dictionary.
How it works
Each word is turned into a list of 100 numbers (a vector) so that words used in similar contexts get similar vectors. Neighbours are the words whose vectors point in the closest direction (cosine similarity, 1.0 = identical). Analogy does arithmetic on meanings: a : b :: c : ? is answered by the word nearest to b − a + c. FastText builds vectors from character pieces, so it also handles word endings and misspellings, and can place words it never saw.
NLP concept
Distributional semantics, word2vec/FastText embeddings, cosine similarity, analogies. FastText scored best of five models (similarity ρ 0.47; 42% of analogies with 2–5-character pieces).

Neural text generation

Phase 3 · Neural language model · LSTM trained on our corpus

What it is
A small neural network that writes Konkani: it continues the sentence you start.
How it works
The text is split into SentencePiece pieces. The LSTM reads them one by one, keeps a memory of what came before, and gives a probability for every possible next piece. One of the 40 most likely pieces is picked at random, added, and the process repeats until the model ends the sentence. Temperature controls the randomness: low = safe and repetitive, high = varied and less sensible.
NLP concept
Recurrent neural networks (LSTM), language modelling, sampling with temperature and top-k. The same model family as early neural machine translation and text generation.
Try this
Click Generate 3 on the newspaper-style prompt, then try 0.5 and 1.2 temperature.
Keep in mind
It learned mostly from newspapers, so it writes like a newspaper, and <NUM> stands for any number. It is small and trained for 25 minutes on a laptop CPU, so sentences are fluent in places but not always meaningful.

Named entity recognition

Phase 3 · Named Entity Recognition · baseline tagger ner_baselines.py

What it is
Finds names in text and labels them: people, places, organisations, dates and other names.
How it works
Two parts. A gazetteer (list of known names) matched even when Konkani adds a case ending, so गोंयांत (in Goa) still matches गोंय. Then rules: up to 3 words after an honorific (डॉ., श्री) or before a respectful verb form (हांणी, हांचे) are a person; number + month + year is a date.
NLP concept
Named entity recognition, BIO tagging, gazetteers and rules as a baseline before machine-learned models (CRF, then MuRIL/IndicBERT).
Try this
Click Find names. Notice what it misses: Devanagari has no capital letters, so unknown person names are hard; that is why this baseline scores F1 ≈ 0.49 and why we are building an annotated dataset.

Phase 1 · The finished corpus · 1.11 million sentences, 10.51 million words

What it is
A search engine over the corpus we built, showing how often a word is used and where.
How it works
Shows the word's frequency rank (rank 1 = most common word) and count from the Phase 1 frequency list, how many sentences contain it, and up to 25 example sentences. The grey label under each sentence is the source document: news_bhaangarbhuin_2026_… is a newspaper issue and its date, lit_… a book, wiki_… Wikipedia.
NLP concept
Corpus, frequency lists, concordance (keyword in context). These counts are what Zipf's law and the coverage analysis are built on.
